Can responsible-AI evaluation use substantially less compute without changing the conclusions we draw from it?

We measure evaluation quality and operational footprint together, rather than treating efficiency as a proxy for trustworthy results.

Findings

Compute savings change what the benchmark tells us

01

Batching is the reliable first lever

Across the evaluated models and benchmarks, it largely preserved quality and usually lowered energy use.

02

INT8 is not an energy shortcut here

It preserved average quality, but raised runtime and energy use in every evaluated setting.

03

Smaller benchmarks save most, with a limit

They gave the most consistent savings, but conclusions became less stable at the smallest sizes.

Interpret within the study’s scope

These findings are specific to the models, benchmarks, and hardware studied. Footprint estimates and judge-based metrics depend on their measurement choices, so results may not transfer unchanged to other settings.
